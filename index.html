// 00 The engine

Eighteen models. One decision every 2 seconds.

Every number ODIN can measure, it measures on your own account: the pool's payout delay, the price cut NiceHash accepts, the fill rate at each price, the regime the market is in and how fast it reverts. Every number it has to estimate carries a calibrated uncertainty into the decision, and every model reports a statistic you can check.

01 · HASHVALUE

Live income per EH/s

86400·R / (2³²·D) · (1−fee) · k

Difficulty and real block rewards, fees included, net of pool and buyer fees.

02 · CALIBRATION

Checked against your pool

k = ΔCredits / ΔModel(t − lag)

Compares what the pool paid with what the model expected, after the payout delay ODIN measures itself.

03 · FORECAST

Retarget ± σ

σ = (1+c)(B/2016)√(1/n + 1/B)

Next difficulty change with its error bar. The bid ceiling glides down ahead of it.

04 · PACING

Budget-aware parking

argmax f·L·(δI − (1+μ)p)

Park price solved for the money you have and how long it must last, by Lagrangian duality.

05 · DIP SPEED

Spend into the dip

L = L₀ + ΔL·clamp((m−m*)/2σ)

Speed climbs only when the margin clears the model's own error band. Changing speed is free.

06 · CUSUM

Change-point alarm

S⁺ = max(0, S⁺ + z − k) > h

Detects when the market jumps to a new level and down-weights stale history immediately.

07 · FILL CURVE

How much really fills

q(gap) = isotonic( served / limit )

Learns the share of each order served at each distance from the clearing price.

08 · VALIDATION

Walk-forward tuning

replay · no look-ahead · CSCV

Replays your own recorded market, and adopts a setting only if it wins out of sample and passes the overfitting test (18).

09 · AUCTION

Priced to win

p* = max(floor, (1+ε)·max bids)

Auction venues are valued at the bid that would actually win, never the listed floor. Unverified prices can't raise an alert.

10 · GPU VALUE

Every card, every coin

v = maxₖ rₖ · min(1, PL/W) · (1−fee)

Each rented card valued per coin from measured hashrate and power, derated for host power limits.

11 · REGIME ● LIVE

Bayesian change-points

P(rₜ|x₁:ₜ) ∝ Σ P(xₜ|r)·P(r|rₜ₋₁)

The full posterior over how long the current market regime has lasted. A Student-t predictive means one spike cannot fool it; a new level that holds cannot hide from it.

Adams & MacKay, 2007
9 confirmed shifts in 72 h · CUSUM fired 22×
12 · DELIVERY ● LIVE

Kalman-filtered pool credit

x̂ ← x̂ + K(z − x̂) · K = P/(P+R)

What your pool really pays, tracked as a hidden state with its own error bar. Noise is learned from the innovations, and the filter's consistency is chi-square tested.

Kalman, 1960 · Mehra, 1970
NIS 0.78 (ideal 1.0)
13 · CONFORMAL ● LIVE

Error bars with a guarantee

αₜ₊₁ = αₜ + γ(α − errₜ)

Distribution-free intervals whose coverage holds for any sequence, even after the market shifts. A dip boost needs the 90% lower bound of its margin to clear, not just the estimate.

Gibbs & Candès, 2021
coverage measured, every forecast
14 · FORECAST ● LIVE

Quantile regression, online

min Σ ρτ(y − xᵀβ) · CQR

The clearing price 30 minutes ahead as a full 10–50–90 distribution, learned online, conformalised, and scored against a random walk on every single forecast.

Koenker & Bassett, 1978 · Romano et al., 2019
81% coverage of 80% · +7.6% skill
15 · REVERSION ● LIVE

Exact first-passage times

T = θ⁻¹∫ √(2π)·exp(u²/2)·Φ(u) du

Ornstein–Uhlenbeck on two time scales separates noise that snaps back in minutes from a regime worth an order. A dip classed as transient noise never gets a paid dip order.

Uhlenbeck & Ornstein, 1930 · Bertram, 2010
half-life 13 min · DF t −5.7
16 · RIVALS ● LIVE

Posterior on a bot's ceiling

Ŝ(x) = Πᵢ (1 − dᵢ/nᵢ)

Every rival bot's ceiling is bracketed from where it walked away, and estimated by product-limit survival for the ones still fighting. ODIN declines wars it is going to lose.

Kaplan & Meier, 1958
learns from every Clore war
17 · WAR GAME ● LIVE

Thompson-sampled bids

θₐ ~ Beta(αₐ, βₐ) · argmax θₐ·h(a)

Learns which jump size actually wins against each market's bots, weighed against the profit headroom it leaves. Discounted so it re-learns when a bot changes behaviour.

Thompson, 1933 · Russo et al., 2018
every war move scored
18 · OVERFIT ● LIVE

Probability of backtest overfitting

PBO = P(λ ≤ 0) over C(8,4) splits

Every candidate setting faces combinatorially symmetric cross-validation, 140 tests. If the in-sample winner is likely a fluke, nothing changes. Most tuners never ask.

Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu, 2017
gate on every --optimize

Live simulations on synthetic data, running the same algorithms ODIN runs on your account.

// 04 Supported hardware · heterogeneous fleets

One telemetry schema. Every class of silicon.

From a single GPU on a desk to an industrial ASIC hall, SLEIPNIR speaks each device's native control plane: cgminer RPC on TCP 4028 for industrial ASICs, the AxeOS HTTP API for open-source boards, miner APIs and hardware sensors for GPUs and CPUs. It normalizes all of them into one schema of hashrate, accepted and rejected shares, thermals, power and efficiency, polled concurrently and fed straight back into ODIN's delivery model.

// Built by an operator

Military discipline. Verifiable cryptography.

ODIN Suite is engineered by Steve Chancellor, a former U.S. Navy Operations Specialist, Search and Rescue swimmer and VBSS (Visit, Board, Search and Seizure) team member. Those jobs run on three rules, and so does every line of ODIN:

  • Own the picture. An Operations Specialist builds the tactical picture before anyone moves. ODIN reads every market every 2 seconds before it commits a sat.
  • Verify, then trust. A boarding team searches before it clears a ship. ODIN reconciles every order against what your pool actually credited.
  • No one left behind. A rescue swimmer never takes a risk the mission can't afford. Kill switches, spend caps and auto-stops protect your capital first.

Keys encrypted at rest

Windows: every API key, secret and password is sealed with DPAPI to your account the first time ODIN starts. Linux: sealed to the machine's TPM2 chip with systemd encrypted credentials, and decrypted in memory for the ODIN service only. Copy the files to another machine and they are useless.

No withdrawals

Use marketplace keys with withdrawals turned off. ODIN never needs to move coins out of your accounts.

Watch-only first

Every install starts in watch-only mode. Nothing trades until you switch it on yourself.

Verified builds

Programs are built by GitHub Actions. Each release ships SHA256 checksums; the Linux installer refuses a mismatch.

Tamper-evident audit ledger

Every decision is written as a SHA-256 hash chain. odin --verify-audit finds any edited, deleted or reordered line.

Offline licences

Ed25519-signed licence files, bound to one machine or a whole site and checked locally. Air-gapped sites never phone home.
